Case study, 4Clubbers, live app and MVP-2

Finding a crew for a club night or festival

4Clubbers is a live app for electronic music fans in Poland. I designed it from scratch: a curated feed, event pages with line-ups, a calendar, a map and ticket links. Every event page counts the people who are looking for a crew, but they had no way to find each other. MVP-2 adds the social features that let people find a crew and talk, with safety tools in every place where they meet. I designed it.

The insight

The looking for a crew counter

I designed the live app for discovery. Each event page shows two counters. One says how many people are going. The other says how many are looking for a crew.

The moderator of the 4Clubbers fan page and group often got questions from people who were looking for someone to go with. The counter showed the same need inside the app. People could say that they were looking for company, but they could not find each other or talk. MVP-2 is my design for these steps.

Find a crew

Find a crew and send a welcome message

The flow starts from the event. A user opens an event, sees the crews and the people who are looking, and sends an invite. The invite includes a short welcome message, so the first contact has some context. When the other person accepts, the talk moves to a private chat.

The event board

One board per event, with filters by intent

Each event has its own board, a group chat and a list of attendees. People post on the board for different reasons, so I added three filters by intent: All, Looking for a crew, Questions. Someone who wants company can see only crew posts. People comment on posts and continue in the group chat.

Safety

Safety tools wherever people meet

In MVP-2 people talk to strangers before a night out, so I treated safety as part of every flow. Report, block and mute sit in the same menu on every post, comment, chat and profile. People do not have to look for them.

Actions with lasting effects, like blocking someone or leaving a chat, open a confirmation sheet first. The sheet says in plain words what will happen.

Blocked users and muted chats are listed in Privacy and Safety. Users can undo a block or a mute there at any time.

States

States for loading, errors and no connection

A message that fails without any sign looks like it was sent. So I designed the states around content and messages: loading, load error, offline, publishing, failed to publish and failed to send. Each state tells the user what happened to their post or message.

Permissions and onboarding

Ask for access only when a feature needs it

The app asks for notifications, photos and the camera only when a feature needs them. Before the system prompt, a short screen explains why.

A short social onboarding introduces the new features before people start using them.

Where it stands

Live app, MVP-2 designed

I designed the live app at 4clubbers.app from scratch. It has the curated feed, event pages with line-ups and counters, a calendar, a map and ticket links. The app has about 5,000 registered accounts.

MVP-2 is designed. The design has 162 screens with the main flows, the safety actions and the states for loading, errors and no connection. I have no usage results for MVP-2.

For me, success would mean that people who mark themselves as looking for a crew send invites and start chats.

What I learned

Every meeting place needs a way out

I learned that every place where people meet in the app also needs a way to report, block or leave.

Next, I would test the crew invite as a clickable prototype with a few fans. I would check if the welcome message helps people reply to an invite.
