Case study · 4Clubbers · Consumer mobile · Live product

Turning “21 people looking for a crew” into a feature.

4Clubbers is a live app for electronic music fans in Poland: festivals, club nights, line-ups and tickets in one place. Every event page already showed how many people were looking for someone to go with, but there was no way to act on it. In MVP-2 I designed the social layer: finding a crew, an event board, group and private chats, and the safety tools that make all of it work.

[01] The insight

People don’t go to festivals alone.

The live product does discovery well: a curated feed, event pages with line-ups, a calendar, a map and ticket links. On every event page two counters sit side by side: how many people are going, and how many are looking for a crew.

That second number was a signal with nowhere to go. You could say you were looking for people, but not find them, talk to them or decide whether to trust them. MVP-2 is built around closing that gap.

[02] The event board

A place to talk about one event, before and during it.

Each event gets its own board and group chat. Posts can be filtered by intent, so “looking for a crew” and “questions” don’t drown in general chatter.

Photos, reactions and replies work the same way on the board, in comments and in chats, so people learn the pattern once.

[03] Key decisions

Social features only work when people feel safe.

Safety by default

Report, block and mute everywhere

Every post, comment, chat and profile has the same menu with report, block and mute, so safety is never more than two taps away.

Clear consequences

Confirm before anything permanent

Deleting a conversation, blocking someone or leaving a chat is confirmed in a sheet that says exactly what will happen.

Honest states

Loading, offline, failed to send

Every screen has a designed state for loading, errors, no connection, publishing and messages that failed to send, because festivals have bad signal.

Permissions in context

Ask when it makes sense

Notifications, photos and camera are requested at the moment they are needed, with a short explanation before the system prompt.

Onboarding

Three screens, then the crowd

A short social onboarding explains crews, the board and safety before the first interaction.

Privacy

Control in one place

Blocked users and muted chats are collected in Privacy & Safety, where any decision can be undone.

[04] Where it stands

What is live and what comes next.

Discovery, event pages, the calendar, the map and ticket links are live in production at 4clubbers.app.

MVP-2 is fully specified: 162 screens covering happy paths, edge cases, moderation and failure states, ready for development.

[05] What I learned

In a social product the feature is the easy part. Trust is the product: the report button, the block confirmation and the offline state decide whether people come back.
