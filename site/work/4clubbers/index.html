Case study, 4Clubbers, consumer mobile app, live product

Making "21 people looking for a crew" a real feature.

4Clubbers is a live app for electronic music fans in Poland. It has festivals, club nights, line-ups and tickets in one place. Every event page already showed how many people were looking for someone to go with, but users could not do anything with this information. For MVP-2 I designed the social features: finding a crew, an event board, group and private chats, and the safety tools that all of this needs.

The insight

Most people go to festivals with friends.

The live product is good at discovery. It has a curated feed, event pages with line-ups, a calendar, a map and ticket links. On every event page there are two counters next to each other: how many people are going, and how many are looking for a crew.

Users could see the second number, but there was nothing they could do with it. You could say you were looking for people, but you could not find them, talk to them or check if you can trust them. MVP-2 was designed to solve this.

The event board

A place to talk about one event, before and during it.

Each event has its own board and group chat. Users can filter posts by type, so "looking for a crew" and "questions" don't get lost in general chat.

Photos, reactions and replies work the same way on the board, in comments and in chats. People learn it once and can use it everywhere.

Key decisions

Safety tools in every part of the app.

Safety by default

Report, block and mute everywhere

Every post, comment, chat and profile has the same menu with report, block and mute. Safety tools are always two taps away or less.

Clear consequences

Confirm before anything permanent

When users delete a conversation, block someone or leave a chat, they see a confirmation sheet that explains exactly what will happen.

Clear states

Loading, offline, failed to send

Every screen has a designed state for loading, errors, no connection, publishing and messages that failed to send. Mobile signal at festivals is often bad.

Permissions in context

Ask at the right moment

The app asks for notifications, photos and camera only when they are needed, with a short explanation before the system prompt.

Onboarding

Three screens before the crowd

A short social onboarding explains crews, the board and safety before the first interaction.

Privacy

All controls in one place

Blocked users and muted chats are listed in Privacy & Safety, where users can undo any decision.

Where it stands

What is live and what is next

Discovery, event pages, the calendar, the map and ticket links are live in production at 4clubbers.app.

MVP-2 is fully specified: 162 screens with main flows, edge cases, moderation and error states, ready for development.

What I learned

I learned that in a social product, building the feature is the easier part. Small things like the report button, the block confirmation and the offline state decide if people trust the app and come back.
