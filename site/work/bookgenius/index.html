Case study, BookGenius, consumer mobile app

Giving users a second chance after they skip the paywall.

BookGenius helps readers remember what they read, with active recall, quizzes, flashcards and audio summaries. I redesigned a moment that is very important for the business: what happens when a new user is asked to start a free trial.

RoleProduct Designer
ScopeUX strategy, flows, UI
PlatformiOS & Android
FocusTrial & conversion
The problem

Users saw the paywall before they saw the value.

Most new users came to the subscription screen before they tried anything in the app. A typical "choose a plan" screen felt like selling, so people closed it. Then they left, or went into the app and never started a trial.

1. How can we ask for a trial so it does not feel like a barrier?

2. What should happen when someone still says "not now"?

Step one

A trial guide in place of a paywall.

On the first screen I explain how the trial works: full access for 7 days, a reminder 2 days before it ends, and cancelling in two taps. The price is still visible, in a small and clear note at the bottom.

My assumption was that people resist less and trust more when they feel in control. "Try it first, we will remind you" is a very different message than "Should I pay?".

Step two

Three options for users who skip the trial.

I prepared three ways to handle people who close the trial screen. I compared them on two things that often conflict: user satisfaction and retention.

Option 1, current behaviour

Soft Entry

Users go to the dashboard, and the trial is promoted with floating CTAs and notifications. People trust it and do not get frustrated. But there are few decision points, so it is easy to postpone or forget the trial.

Option 2, recommended for testing

Value Wall

A one-time offer before the dashboard: 50% off for 3 months, a free month, or free access for inviting friends. There is one clear moment to decide, and it is easy to A/B test on exactly the users who skip.

Option 3

Value Tease

Users can explore the app, but key features are marked as available after starting the trial. Users see real value without pressure. The balance is hard though. If we lock too much, people get frustrated. If we lock too little, the preview is enough for them.

The decision

A/B test the Value Wall against the current flow.

I recommended an A/B test of the Value Wall against Soft Entry. It focuses on exactly the group we were losing. Users who used to leave without a word now make a choice we can measure. Nothing changes for users who start the trial.

I wanted it to stay respectful: one offer, shown only once, with a clear "no thanks" button. The copy presents it as a gift, and there is a referral version for people who will not pay but will share the app.

What I learned

I learned that the paywall works more like a conversation with the user. Designing for the person who says "not now" was as important as designing for the person who says "yes".
