Case study · BookGenius · Consumer mobile app

Turning a skipped paywall into a second chance to convert.

BookGenius helps readers remember what they read through active recall, quizzes, flashcards and audio summaries. I redesigned the moment that decides the business: what happens when a new user is asked to start a free trial.

RoleProduct Designer
ScopeUX strategy, flows, UI
PlatformiOS & Android
FocusTrial & conversion
[01] The problem

Users met the paywall before they met the value.

Most new users reached the subscription screen before they had experienced anything the app does. A classic “choose a plan” screen felt like a sales push, so people closed it and either left or wandered into the app without ever starting a trial.

1. How do we ask for a trial without it feeling like a barrier?

2. What should happen when someone still says “not now”?

[02] Step one

A trial guide instead of a paywall.

Instead of plans and prices, the first screen explains how the trial works: full access for 7 days, a reminder 2 days before it ends, and cancelling in two taps. The price is there, but as a calm, transparent footnote.

The assumption behind it: when people feel in control, resistance drops and trust grows. “Try it first, we’ll remind you” is a very different message from “Should I pay?”.

[03] Step two

Three strategies for users who skip the trial.

I mapped three ways to handle people who close the trial screen and compared them on two things that pull in opposite directions: user satisfaction and retention.

Option 1 · existing behaviour

Soft Entry

Users land on the dashboard and the trial is promoted with floating CTAs and notifications. High trust and no frustration, but with few decision points activation is easy to postpone or forget.

Option 2 · recommended for testing

Value Wall

A one-time offer before the dashboard: 50% off for 3 months, a free month or free access for inviting friends. One clear decision moment, easy to A/B test on exactly the users who skip.

Option 3

Value Tease

Users explore the app, but key features show as available after starting the trial. Real value and no pressure, but a hard balance: lock too much and it frustrates, too little and the teaser is enough.

[04] The decision

Test the Value Wall against what we had.

I recommended an A/B test of the Value Wall against Soft Entry. It targets exactly the group we were losing and turns a silent exit into a measurable choice, without changing anything for users who start the trial.

To keep it respectful: one offer, shown once, with a clear “no thanks”. Copy framed as a gift, and a referral variant for people who won’t pay but will share.

[05] What I learned

A paywall is not a screen, it is a conversation. Designing for the person who says “not now” mattered as much as designing for the one who says “yes”.
